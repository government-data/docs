<Aside type="caution" title="Example data">
  The figures in this section are made up. They show how a quality report reads and what each chart
  answers; they are not any organisation's data.
</Aside>

The first question of a report is whether quality is improving. Each run is a
cut; comparing cuts shows the trend, where customers moved and which offices
pulled up or down.

<Chart id="igcd-trend" />

**How to read it.** Conformity counts rule checks; the IGCD (overall data quality
index) is the mean of every customer's data reliability index (ICD), which weighs severity, field priority and the data's
age. Both rising is good. Conformity steady while the IGCD falls usually
means data going out of date: an update campaign, rather than fixes case by
case.

<Chart id="band-transitions" />

**How to read it.** Customers above the diagonal improved their band; below it,
they worsened. A large flow from high to medium usually means data expiring
(timeliness), not new errors.

<Chart id="groups-changed" />

**How to read it.** The change of the mean ICD of each office's customers. It
points to where to look, not to blame: an office may drop because it received
customers from another one.
