Everything in Quality Data follows one chain, each step handing its output
to the next: **source → dataset and mapping → rules → cut → evaluation →
results and indicators → dashboard → remediation queue**.

<FlowDiagram {...chain("en")} />

This page walks it once with one made-up example: a `customers` table in
the organisation's customer database, with 12,000 records and the columns
`CustomerId`, `Email`, `Region` and `LastUpdate`.

## Source

A **source** is a database the organisation already runs. Quality Data reads
it and never writes to it; today it reads PostgreSQL, MySQL and SQL Server.
A source is added under **Sources** with its server and credentials, which
are stored sealed and never shown again; its tables then appear in the
**Explorer**. _Example:_ the customer database becomes a source, and
`customers` is one of its tables. **Next:** that table is mapped. More in
[Connect a source](../connect-a-source/).

## Dataset and mapping

A **dataset** is one table of a source as Quality Data reads it: a name, its
fields and the rules that check them. Its **mapping** says how the table is
read: which column each field reads and its type, the **record key** that
tells one record from another, each field's **priority** (critical, high,
medium or low), and the **roles** of some fields, such as a **grouping
field** (an office, a region, a channel) that the dashboard and the queues
split results by.

**Map this table**, a button inside a table opened in the Explorer,
samples the table, proposes the mapping and some first rules, and
publishes them. The sample
keeps counts, never values. _Example:_ the dataset `customers`, with
`CustomerId` as its record key (the table's primary key) and `Region`
proposed as a grouping field from its name. **Next:** rules check its
fields. More in [Map a table into a
dataset](../map-a-table/).

## Rules

A **rule** applies a **pattern** to a field: `required` (it has a value),
`format` (it has the right shape), `fresh` (a date is recent enough) and
eleven more. The pattern gives the rule its **dimension**, the kind of
quality it measures (completeness, validity, timeliness…), and a default
**severity** (blocking, major or minor). A rule is a few lines of text,
written in the rule editor, which checks it as you type and says it in
words under **As read**.

_Example:_ three rules for `customers`:

```yaml
dataset: customers
rules:
  - { id: EML-01, field: Email, check: required }
  - { id: EML-02, field: Email, check: format, named: email }
  - { id: UPD-01, field: LastUpdate, check: fresh, max_age: 24 months }
```

The rules that count are the ones on **`main`**, the approved version of a
dataset's rules. Someone allowed to approve takes a change there with
**Deploy**; anyone else uses **Save as a draft**, and the **draft** waits
for an approver to **Approve** or **Discard** it. **History** keeps every
change, and **Test** runs a file on the latest cut before it counts.
**Next:** every cut is measured with the rules on `main`. More in the
[tutorial](../rule-tutorial/), [Rule patterns](../rule-patterns/) and
[Take a change to main](../rules-to-main/).

## Cut

A **cut** is the dataset's data as it was on one date. Quality Data copies
the table from the source into its own store, the **lake**, so the cut can
be measured again later without touching the source. A dataset has at most
one cut per date, started with **Run now** under the dataset's **Cuts** or
by a **schedule** (daily, weekly, monthly…). _Example:_ the cut of 31
October 2026 holds the 12,000 customers as they were that day. **Next:**
once the copy is complete, its evaluation starts on its own. More in [Run
and schedule cuts](../run-cuts/).

## Evaluation

The **evaluation** runs every rule on `main` on every record of the cut. For
each record a rule gives one of three results: it **fails**, it **passes**,
or it **does not apply** (the record is outside the rule's condition, or the
value it checks is empty). A record a rule does not apply to is left out of
that rule's figures: it is not a pass.

_Example:_ 300 customers have no e-mail. `EML-01` fails those 300 and passes
the other 11,700. `EML-02` does not apply to the 300; of the 11,700 it
applies to, 150 fail. `UPD-01` fails every customer whose `LastUpdate` is
before 31 October 2024.

Two fixed ideas lie under every figure:

- **Empty** means no value, an empty text, or only spaces, including the
  invisible no-break space. Only `required` (and its opposite, `empty`)
  looks at empty values; every other pattern skips them, so a missing value
  counts once, not once per rule.
- **Dates are compared with the cut, never with today.** "Within 24 months"
  means 24 months before the cut's date, so measuring the October 2026 cut
  again next year gives the same result.

More, such as how text is compared, in [Rule language: what Quality Data
decides](../rule-language/#what-quality-data-decides-for-every-rule).

## Results and indicators

The **results** are each rule's counts at the cut: applied, passed, failed.
From them come the **indicators**:

- **Conformity** of a rule: the share of the records it applies to that
  pass. `EML-02`: 1 − 150 / 11,700, about 98.7 %.
- **ICD** (data reliability index): a score from 0 to 1 for each field of
  each record, lowered by the rules that fail on it, weighted by severity,
  and, for values that go out of date such as an e-mail, by the time since
  the last update. A record's ICD weights its fields by priority.
- **IGCD** (overall data quality index): the mean ICD of the dataset at the
  cut, the one figure to follow over time.

More in [From results to indicators](../indicators/) and [Formulas](../formulas/).

## Dashboard

Each dataset has a **Dashboard**, under the dataset in the Explorer and in
the **Quality** tab of a table. It charts the results of the **Cut** you
choose: conformity and IGCD by cut, failures by dimension, the fields with
the most failures and more. A chart appears only when the mapping gives it
what it needs; charts by group need a grouping field. It shows totals,
never a customer's values. **Next:** the failures, grouped into queues.
More in [Reading the dashboard](../charts/).

## Remediation queue

A **finding** is a record that fails a rule at a cut. A **remediation
queue** gathers the findings of one group of a grouping field, such as
`Region` North, with a **service level agreement (SLA)**: the days allowed
to fix them, 30 unless someone who may edit the dataset sets another under
**Remediation SLAs**, below the dashboard. The **Remediation queues** chart
shows each group's open findings, the overdue ones and their summed
priority.

Data is fixed where it lives, in the source system. A finding is resolved
when a later cut no longer fails it, so a fix shows at the next evaluated
cut, and the chain starts again. In the example, the customers failing
`EML-01`, `EML-02` or `UPD-01` fall into the queue of their region.

Today the queues show counts only: the product does not yet list a queue's
records, nor carry findings from one cut to the next, so **Overdue** and
**Resolved at this cut** read 0. More in [Reading the dashboard: remediation
queues](../charts/#remediation-queues).
