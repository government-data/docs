Every indicator Quality Data shows comes from one source: the result of
each rule on each record of a cut. This page follows that result up to the
figures people read, and says what each figure answers and what to do
about it. The exact formulas are in [Formulas](../formulas/); where each
figure appears on the dashboard, in [Reading the dashboard](../charts/).

## Rule results

For each record, a rule **fails**, **passes** or **does not apply**. A
record the rule does not apply to is left out of that rule's figures: it is
neither a pass nor a failure. That keeps a missing value from counting once
per rule, and keeps a rule written for some records (companies, closed
accounts) from looking better because of the records it ignores. More in
[Concepts: evaluation](../concepts/#evaluation).

## Conformity

A rule's **conformity** is the share of the records it applies to that
pass. It answers how well each requirement is met, and it is the figure to
review rules with: a rule with very low conformity means either that the
data is wrong or that the rule is badly posed. Overall conformity, on the
trend by cut, is the share of all rule checks that passed.

Conformity counts checks, not records, and treats every rule alike. The
ICD weighs what matters.

## The ICD of a field

The **ICD** (data reliability index) is a score from 0 to 1 for each field
of each record. It joins up to three components, each telling a different
reason why a value is not reliable:

| Component           | It comes from                                     | When it is low, the value…                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **C** · conformity  | The rules that read only this field               | is wrong by itself: empty, badly formed, repeated |
| **S** · consistency | The rules that read this field with others        | contradicts another field                         |
| **A** · timeliness  | The time from the record's update date to the cut | may be right, but is old and trusted less         |

C and S weight each failing rule by its severity: a blocking failure costs
more than a minor one. A loses half its value for each year without an
update, and applies only to fields whose value goes out of date, such as an
e-mail or an address, and only when the dataset has an update date. A
component with nothing to measure, such as no rule applying, leaves the
score, and the others share its weight.

## The ICD of a record and of an entity

A record's ICD averages its fields' ICD, weighting each field by its
**priority**: a critical field weighs four times a low one.

An **entity** is whoever the records are about, such as a customer or a
company, known by the field holding the entity key. When one entity has
several records, its ICD takes, for each field, the mean of its records,
except for critical fields, where it takes the worst: one bad identity
document is enough to doubt the entity. Without an entity key, the figures
stop at records.

## The IGCD

The **IGCD** (overall data quality index) is the mean ICD of the dataset at
a cut: over its entities when it has an entity key, otherwise over its
records. It is the one figure to follow over time, on the **Conformity and
IGCD by cut** chart. A rise means the data became more reliable; a fall
with no new errors usually means data going out of date.

## Bands

Each entity falls into a **band** by its ICD:

| Band | ICD         | What it says                                                  |
| ---- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Good | ≥ 0.90      | The record can be relied on                                   |
| Fair | 0.75 – 0.90 | Usable, with some fields to fix or update at the next contact |
| Poor | < 0.75      | Too many problems to rely on without checking                 |

Bands make the ICD actionable. An organisation decides what each band
means for its own processes, for example which records an update campaign
goes to first, or which segment needs its capture form reviewed. A tall
pile of entities just below a limit is cheap to move: a few fixes per
entity change their band. Bands need an entity key.

## Priority

A finding's **priority** says which failures cost most: the weight of the
field it fails (its priority over four) times what the field's ICD lacks
from 1. A failure on a critical field that is already doubtful comes before
a minor one on a reliable field. Each remediation queue sums its open
findings' priority, so a queue with few but costly findings stands out.

## Reading an indicator

| Indicator                 | The question it answers                                                                            | The decision it supports                                              |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Conformity of a rule      | How well is each requirement met?                                                                  | Review the rule or the data; tune rules with their authors            |
| ICD of a field            | Which data elements are least reliable, and why?                                                   | Fix a field with low C or S; update one with low A                    |
| ICD and band of an entity | How reliable is the whole record of this customer or company?                                      | Where an update campaign or a check goes first                        |
| IGCD                      | Is the dataset's quality improving?                                                                | Follow the trend cut by cut; confirm that fixes held                  |
| Priority                  | Which findings cost most?                                                                          | The order in which a queue is worked                                  |
| p′ chart by group         | Does this group fail more than normal, or is it noise?                                             | Look for a cause at the point of capture only where there is a signal |
| Outliers by group         | Is this amount unusual for its group?                                                              | Review it: fix the errors, keep the legitimate values                 |
| PSI by deciles            | Did the population change since the previous cut? The population stability index (PSI) measures it | Review a moved population before using it                             |

Every figure is read at the cut it belongs to, and a chart that compares
compares with the evaluated cut before it. What the indicators do not
capture is in [Assumptions and limits](../assumptions/).

Organisations in a regulated sector may also follow an indicator their
supervisor measures: see the optional [module for regulated financial
entities in Colombia](../sector-colombia-finance/).
