## What mapping decides

Mapping turns one table of a source into a **dataset**. Its mapping says:

- **The fields:** one per column of the table, named as the column, each
  with its type (text, number, date…).
- **The record key:** the field or fields that tell one record from
  another, taken from the table's primary key. Without a record key (or an
  entity key), failing records cannot be followed, and the remediation
  queues stay empty.
- **Each field's priority:** critical, high, medium or low, which weights
  the field in a record's ICD (data reliability index). Mapping sets every
  field to medium, so all weigh the same.
- **The roles of some fields**, which switch charts and queues on.

## Field roles

A role tells Quality Data what a field is for. Mapping proposes them from
the column's name and its sample: a role proposed for the wrong column, or
missed, changes which charts appear.

| Role           | What it is                                                                                                | Proposed for                                                                                                                      | Switches on                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Grouping field | A field whose values are groups that fix their own data: an office, a region, a channel                   | A text column named like _office_, _branch_, _advisor_, _product_, _channel_, _region_ or _zone_, with up to 500 different values | The **Remediation queues**, one per value, and the charts by group: **p′ chart by group**, **Largest groups**, **Groups that changed most** |
| Entity key     | Who the record is about, such as a person's or company's identity document                                | A column that looks like an identity document, or named like _document type_                                                      | **Distribution of the ICD by entity** and **How entities moved between bands**                                                              |
| Segment        | A kind of customer or product, with few values                                                            | A text column named like _client type_ or _segment_, with up to 12 values                                                         | With an entity key, **Data condition by segment**                                                                                           |
| Update date    | When the record was last updated                                                                          | A date column named like _update_ or _modified_                                                                                   | **Update recency**                                                                                                                          |
| Outlier amount | An amount where unusual values are sought                                                                 | A number column named like _income_, _amount_, _balance_ or _value_                                                               | With a grouping field, **Outliers by group**                                                                                                |
| Drift variable | A number whose population is compared from one cut to the next, with the PSI (population stability index) | A number column named like _income_, _age_, _rate_ or _term_                                                                      | **PSI by deciles**                                                                                                                          |

The names are recognised in English and Spanish. Entity key, segment,
update date and outlier amount go to one field at most: the first column
that fits. Two more roles belong to an optional sector indicator and are
never proposed. Charts that need no role appear for every dataset.

Mapping also marks fields whose value goes out of date, such as an e-mail,
a phone, an address, an income or an employer, by their names: the time
since the last update lowers their ICD.

## To map a table

1. In the **Explorer**, open the source, then the schema, until you see
   the table, marked **not read**.
2. Open the table and choose **Map this table**.
3. In **Map a table of …**, check **Table** (`schema.table`, as the source
   names it) and type the **Dataset** name: lowercase letters, digits and
   `_`, starting with a letter, such as `customers`. The name cannot be
   changed later.
4. Choose **Map and collect**.

**You should see** the notice _Mapping customers: you will be told when it
is ready._ and a turning circle beside the table; point at it to read the
step it is on and since when: waiting for a worker, sampling the table,
collecting its data, evaluating the rules. When it is done, a notice says
_customers is ready: 12,000 records evaluated._ It stays until you close
it.

The notices come only while Quality Data is open in your browser. If you
close it, nothing is lost: the dataset appears under **Lake** once it is
published, and its first cut under its **Cuts**.

## What the proposal does

1. It reads a **sample** of 10,000 records of the table and keeps counts:
   how many are empty, how many different values, lengths. It never keeps a
   value.
2. It proposes the mapping: the fields and their types, the record key, the
   roles, the priorities.
3. It proposes rules for each field: `required` when no record of the
   sample is empty and the column does not allow empty values; `unique` on
   the primary key; `format` for a column that looks like an e-mail, a
   mobile number or an identity document. Each is named `MAP-001`,
   `MAP-002`… with severity major.
4. It **publishes** the mapping and those rules directly on `main`, the
   approved version: there is no draft to approve.
5. It takes the **first cut**, today's, and evaluates it with those rules.

## To see what was proposed

- **The dataset:** under **Lake** in the Explorer, as **◆ customers**, with
  the source and table it reads. Open it: a line per column, with its rules
  under it; a column with none is marked **no rules**.
- **The first cut:** under the dataset's **Cuts**, **Evaluated**, with its
  records.
- **The grouping fields:** on the dataset's **Dashboard**, below the
  charts, **Remediation SLAs** (the service level agreements, the days each
  queue has) shows one block per grouping field. If it says **The dataset's mapping
  has no field with the dimension role: its findings have no queue.**, none
  was proposed (the form calls a grouping field a `dimension`).
- **The other roles:** by the charts on the **Dashboard**; each chart
  appears only when its role is there ([Reading the
  dashboard](../charts/#before-you-read-a-chart)).

No screen shows the mapping itself yet: its roles, priorities and key are
seen only through what they switch on.

## What you can change afterwards

- **The rules:** write, change and remove them as with any rule; see the
  [tutorial](../rule-tutorial/) and [Write, change and test a
  rule](../write-a-rule/).
- **New columns:** when the source's table gains columns, open the dataset
  after **List again** on the source: the new ones show as **not read**.
  **Map the whole table**, under the dataset, adds them as fields, at once,
  ready for rules, with no role and medium priority.
- **The roles, the priorities, the record key and the types** cannot be
  changed yet: there is no screen for it. Mapping the table again proposes
  the same, so it does not help. If something in the mapping is wrong, ask
  whoever supports your installation.

## To remove a dataset

1. In the Explorer, right-click the dataset (**◆ customers** under
   **Lake**, or its table in the source) and choose **Delete**.
2. Read what goes, type the dataset's name under **To confirm, type**, and
   choose **Delete**.

The mapping and the rules leave `main` (the rules catalog's history keeps
them); the schedules, cuts, results and the dataset's copy in the lake are
deleted. The source's table is not touched and can be mapped again. It
cannot be undone, and it is refused while one of its cuts is being
extracted or evaluated.

## Problems

| What you see                                                                                | What it means and what to do                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No **Map this table** on the table                                                          | A dataset reads it already: the table shows **→** and the dataset's name.                                                                                            |
| **There is a dataset with that name already.**                                              | Choose another name.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Check the table, the dataset or the sample.**                                             | The name is not valid, the table is not in the source's last listing (**List again** on the source, and try again), or your policies do not let you manage datasets. |
| **… could not be mapped.**                                                                  | The sample could not be read; nothing was published. Check the source's connection ([Connect a source](../connect-a-source/#to-check-the-connection)) and map again. |
| **…'s data could not be collected.** or **…'s rules could not be evaluated.**               | The dataset was published, but its first cut failed. See [When a run fails](../run-cuts/#when-a-run-fails).                                                          |
| The circle stays on _queued, waiting for a worker_                                          | No worker is taking jobs. Tell your administrator.                                                                                                                   |
| **… was not deleted: one of its cuts is being collected or evaluated. Wait for it to end.** | Wait until the cut is **Evaluated** or **Failed**, then delete again.                                                                                                |
| **Not deleted: the name typed is not ….**                                                   | Type the dataset's name exactly.                                                                                                                                     |
