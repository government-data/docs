A financial entity supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de
Colombia (SFC), the country's financial supervisor, has to show the
quality of its customer data in the terms the supervisor measures. Quality
Data computes that indicator from the same rule results as every other
figure, and has a few formats for Colombian data.

## The SFC indicator

The supervisor's indicator looks at a list of mandatory fields over the
**active** customers. Each field is a **cell**:

- A record is **right** in a cell when the field is filled and no rule
  that reads the field fails. A rule that does not apply leaves it right.
- The cell is **1** when at least 90 % of the active records are right in
  it, and **0** otherwise.
- The **SFC indicator** is the share of cells at 1, for example 7 of 10.

The indicator is all or nothing: a cell at 89.9 % is worth the same as one
at 10 %. That is why each cell also has two counts:

- **Missing**: how many records must be fixed for a failing cell to pass,
  the 90 % of the active records, rounded up, minus those right.
- **Margin**: how many records a passing cell can lose before it fails, the
  records right minus that same 90 %.

The exact list of fields and what counts as active are set by the
supervisor's current guide and circular: check them with your compliance
team before relying on the figure.

### To switch it on

The indicator needs two roles in a dataset's mapping: the **indicator
field** role on each mandatory field, and the **active record** role on the
field that says whether a customer is active, with the values that mean
active. Mapping a table never proposes them, and no screen sets them yet:
ask whoever supports your installation. Without an active-record field,
every record counts as active. The **SFC indicator** chart appears on the
dashboard of a dataset once one field holds the indicator role.

## Reading the SFC indicator chart

**Question:** would we pass what the supervisor measures, and where is it
cheapest to improve? **What you see:** a dot per cell at its share of
active records right. The line at **90 %** is the threshold; the shaded
**80–90 %** band is the _top priority_. Green ✓ dots pass, red ✕ dots fail.
The note gives the cells that pass. The table adds each cell's **missing**,
**margin** and **closeness**.

**How to read it.** Red dots inside the band are where the fewest fixes
turn a cell from 0 to 1: start there, and use _missing_ to size the effort.
Green dots just above 90 % are at risk: watch their margin. The example
uses made-up figures.

## The SFC indicator and the ICD

Both come from the same rule results, and they answer different questions.
The SFC indicator is **binary** and serves to **supervise**: it says
whether each cell passes the threshold, the way the supervisor compares
entities. The ICD (data reliability index) is **continuous** and serves to
**manage**: it says how much each field and each customer's record lacks,
and where to improve. Follow both: the ICD to plan the work, the indicator
to know the result before the supervisor's report. How the ICD is built is
in [From results to indicators](../indicators/).

## Closeness to the threshold and priority

A finding's priority in the remediation queues is the field's weight times
what its ICD lacks, times its **closeness** to the supervisor's threshold.
A field that is not a cell gets the lowest closeness, 0.2. For a cell, by its share of
active records right:

| Share right    | Closeness | Why                                        |
| -------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 80 % to 90 %   | 1.0       | The fewest fixes turn the cell from 0 to 1 |
| 70 % to 80 %   | 0.6       | Within reach with more effort              |
| Below 70 %     | 0.3       | Far from the threshold                     |
| 90 % to 92 %   | 0.5       | Passes, at risk of falling                 |
| 92 % and above | 0.2       | Safe                                       |

So in a dataset with cells, findings on fields close to the threshold go
first. The rest of the priority is in [Formulas: priority](../formulas/#15-priority-and-remediation-queues).

## Formats for Colombian data

`format` takes three named formats for Colombian data, beside the general
ones in [Rule patterns](../rule-patterns/#format):

| `named`      | Accepts                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `mobile_co`  | A Colombian mobile number: `3` and nine digits                                                                                          |
| `divipola`   | A DIVIPOLA code (the national statistics office's political-administrative division): five digits for a municipality, eight for a place |
| `address_co` | A street address: a road type (`CALLE`, `CRA`, `AV`…) and a number                                                                      |

```yaml
- id: MOB-01
  field: Mobile
  check: format
  named: mobile_co
  alert: Not a Colombian mobile number
```

## Rules for anti-money-laundering data

The anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing system (SARLAFT)
makes some fields critical: give them a **critical** priority, so they
weigh most in the ICD and in the queues. A politically exposed person (PEP)
is a typical cross-field rule:

```yaml
- id: PEP-01
  field: PepPost
  check: implies
  if: IsPep = 'YES'
  then: not is_empty(PepPost)
  both_ways: true
  alert: A politically exposed person needs a post, and only they have one
```

Codes from national catalogs, such as the economic activity code (CIIU,
the International Standard Industrial Classification as adapted for
Colombia) or a DIVIPOLA code, are best checked against the catalog itself:
map the catalog as a dataset, declare it as a reference in the [global
rules](../share-rules/), and check the field with `domain`. Map such codes
as text, or they lose their leading zeros (`QR024`).

```yaml
references:
  activities: { dataset: ciiu_codes, key: Code }
```

```yaml
- id: ACT-01
  field: ActivityCode
  check: domain
  in: { reference: activities.Code }
  alert: The economic activity is not in the CIIU catalog
```

## Bands and due diligence

Quality Data puts each customer in a band (good, fair or poor) by their
ICD. A supervised entity may tie the bands to its own processes, such as
simplified updates for the good band and enhanced due diligence for the
poor one. That decision belongs to the entity: the product computes the
band, and does not act on it.
