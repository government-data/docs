**For** the person who prepares the data and writes the rules. **At the
end** one table of a source is a dataset, measured at a first cut, with a
rule of yours deployed and its result on the dashboard. The example is the
`customers` table of [Concepts](../concepts/).

## Before you start

- An account with the `quality-steward` policy (see
  [Getting started: administrator](../start-admin/)).
- The **Explorer** in the side bar shows **Sources**. If it stays empty and
  says **Could not load. Try again.**, the installation cannot measure data
  yet: tell your administrator.
- The source's server, port, database name, and a user that can read the
  table. A read-only user is best: Quality Data never writes to a source.

## 1. Add the source

In the **Explorer**, choose **+** beside **Sources** (**New source**). Type a
**Name** (lowercase letters, digits and `_`), pick the kind of database
under **Engine**, fill in **Server**, **Port**, **Database**, **User** and
**Password**, and choose **Save**.

**You should see** the source in the Explorer. Open it: a moment later it
lists its schemas and tables, and says how many tables it read and when. If
it says the source did not accept the connection, choose **Edit** on it and
check the server, port and credentials. The password is stored sealed and
never shown again.

## 2. Map a table

Open the table, `customers`, and choose **Map this table**. In **Map a table
of …**, check **Table**, type the **Dataset** name, such as `customers`, and
choose **Map and collect**.

**You should see** a notice that the mapping started, and the table marked
while Quality Data samples it, proposes the mapping and some first rules,
publishes them, copies the data and evaluates it. When it is done, a notice
says _customers is ready_ with the number of records evaluated. The sample
keeps counts, never values.

## 3. Look at the first cut

The dataset now appears under **Lake** in the Explorer, as **◆ customers**.
Open it: its fields with the rules proposed for each, then **Cuts**,
**Schedules** and **Dashboard**. Under **Cuts**, today's cut is
**Evaluated**, with its records. You did not have to start it: mapping takes
the first cut.

The mapping also proposed a grouping field from the column names, such as
`Region`. There is no screen yet to change the mapping afterwards; if
something is wrong in it, ask whoever supports your installation.

## 4. Write a first rule

Follow the [tutorial](../rule-tutorial/) on your dataset: choose **+**
beside a field (**Add a rule to …**), write the rule, read it under **As
read**, use **Test** to run it on the latest cut, and choose **Deploy** to
take it to `main`. The tutorial's example dataset differs from yours; its
steps are the same. Without the right to approve, use **Save as a draft**;
someone who may approve reviews it from the field's **History**, under
**Open drafts** ([Take a change to main](../rules-to-main/#to-review-a-draft)).

## 5. Measure again and see the result

A cut is measured with the rules on `main` when it is taken, so your new
rule counts from the next cut. Under the dataset's **Cuts**, set **If
today's cut is there** to **Replace it (unless running)** and choose **Run
now**.

**You should see** today's cut at the top of **Cuts**, **Extracting**. The
list does not refresh by itself: reload the page and open **Cuts** again
until it reads **Evaluating**, then **Evaluated**. Then open **Dashboard**, keep the latest **Cut**, and find
your rule in **Rules with the lowest conformity**; the
[dashboard guide](../charts/#rules-with-the-lowest-conformity) explains it.
